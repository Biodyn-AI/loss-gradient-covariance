\section{Scalar Risk Bounds Inside an Interacting Matrix Family}
\label{app:robust}

Let the bounded scalar and vector random variables $(h,e)$ satisfy
$0<h_{\min}\leq h\leq h_{\max}$, individual coordinate sign invariance
conditional on $h$, and identical coordinate pairs $(h,e_i)$. Independently
let $B$ be a bounded centered symmetric matrix, $\|B\|_{\rm op}\leq b$.
Take $A=hI+\delta B$ with fixed positive $\delta$ satisfying
\[
 \delta b<h_{\min},\qquad \delta bC_*/\epsilon<\lambda.
\]
Then sample matrices are uniformly positive definite. The exact row identity
and independence give
\[
 J_{ik}=\ind\{i=k\}\E[h\Psi(|e_i|)]
              +\delta\E B_{ik}\E\Psi(|e_i|)
       =\chi\ind\{i=k\}.
\]
At zero parameters the update uses only $e$. Its coordinate-sign
symmetries and common coordinate distributions give fluctuation
covariance $DI$.

The history quotient has global Lipschitz constant $C_*/\epsilon$:
in the derivative representation in Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}, the
multiplier $I-r(z\otimes z)/(r+\epsilon)$ has operator norm at most one.
Let $F_\delta$ be the frozen field and $F_0$ the field with $B$ removed,
using the same primitive histories. Applying the quotient bound in each
coordinate and summing squares yields
\[
 \|f_\delta(u,Z)-f_0(u,Z)\|^2
 \leq\frac{\delta^2C_*^2}{\epsilon^2}
                 \sum_jd_j\|B_{-j}u\|^2
 \leq\frac{\delta^2b^2C_*^2}{\epsilon^2}\|u\|^2.
\]
There is no dimension factor. Taking expectations gives the same bound
on the averaged field difference. The scalar drift points toward zero, giving $u_iF_{0,i}(u_i)\geq0$. Hence
\[
 u^\top G_\delta(u)\geq
        (\lambda-\delta bC_*/\epsilon)\|u\|^2.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:regular-transfer} with ordinary squared distance as the metric. Its
limiting drift is $(\lambda+\chi)I$ and covariance
$D I/[2(\lambda+\chi)]$. Since $\E A=\mu I$, $\mu=\E h$,
\[
 \lim\frac{\E L(u)}\eta=\frac{d\mu D}{4(\lambda+\chi)}.
\]
The fixed perturbation therefore leaves the full leading scalar risk
interval unchanged within this family.

For $e_i=RS_i$ with independent fair signs conditional on $(h,R)$, fixing
the complete separate $h,R,B$ distributions and changing only the
dependence between $h,R$ fixes
the complete matrix-Hessian and vector-noise laws. The covariance between gradients of the same sampled loss at two inputs is
\[
 \Cov(g(u),g(v))=\operatorname{Var}(h)uv^\top
        +\delta^2\E[Buv^\top B]+\E R^2I.
\]
All mixed terms vanish by the signs, centering and independence. The scalar
sharp risk endpoints and all intermediate values remain exactly attainable,
with total risk multiplied by dimension. This does not classify arbitrary
matrix/noise dependence outside the independent-$B$ family. The SGD
covariance equation in Appendix~\ref{app:reference} also gives exact
equality of its complete stationary covariance across this family.

Noncommuting symmetric matrices cannot all be diagonalized in one shared
coordinate system. To make this explicit in dimension two, $B$ can be
equally likely
$\pm\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}$ and
$\pm\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$. The two matrix products in opposite orders differ, so their commutator
is nonzero; off-diagonal samples couple coordinates. Alternatively a
centered radial smooth bump on a Frobenius ball in $\operatorname{Sym}_d$
gives a density in all symmetric-matrix coordinates and almost-sure
noncommutation of
independent samples when $d\geq2$. The perturbation may have to be small;
it is positive and fixed before $\eta$ decreases. The bounds are not claimed to hold uniformly as damping vanishes or
dimension grows.
